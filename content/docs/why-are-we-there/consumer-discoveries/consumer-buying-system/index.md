## What it is:

The Consumer Buying System (CBS) is a tool which describes six stages consumers may go through when making a buying decision.

The time frame for this process will vary enormously from category to category, for example, buying a car could take the consumer a year, whereas buying a box of cereal, from running out of the previous box to the next purchase, could take just hours. Thus some stages are less relevant to some categories, and not all consumers within one specific category may pass through all the stages. For example, when "buying" a new long-distance phone service, some consumers are simply stimulated to change their phone company by an advertised offer, whereas others will collect and evaluate information on long-distance providers to improve their comfort level with the company.

The objective is to take your brand, examine the stages in the CBS and ask, "How can we move the consumer through the process?" This involves examining what the consumer is doing, thinking and feeling and also the contact opportunities for the brand in relation to different communications vehicles, for example, traditional advertising, direct mail, sponsorship, point-of-sale (POS), interactive media, etc.

It is worth noting that with some brands at some stages sociological and economic factors outside our control come into play.

## What its for:

The CBS is useful in that it...

- identifies and prioritizes the brand's target audience(s)...
- helps position the brand...
- helps fill in the Key Discoveries...
- identifies contact points and therefore communication vehicles which are later used in the Total Communications Plan (TCP).

## How to do it:

Each stage should be investigated separately. It is helpful to use consumer research to give insight into how consumers go about buying. This is best done qualitatively using short individual interviews. Don't be restricted by normal 'formal' techniques. You can often find out a considerable amount by asking people yourself, by having on location interviews (at point of purchase or choice) or by talking to recent buyers about their actions, beliefs and feelings at each stage.

The following formats are helpful in setting down a buying system, but you can bring this to life if you use visual or audio-visual material (such as pictures and quotes from people or a camcorder video of someone going through the stages).
