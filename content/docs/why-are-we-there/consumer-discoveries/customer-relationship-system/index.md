## What it is:

The Customer Relationship System (CRS) is a tool to help give insight into the five stages customers may go through when experiencing a relationship with a brand over time.

The CRS usually applies to service brands (such as telecommunications, banks, hotels, etc.) or durables (such as cars, washing machines, stereos) which are acquired on an infrequent basis and tend to be high-involvement categories (as opposed to packaged goods-bought frequently and being low involvement).

The objective is to increase the loyalty bond between the brand and the customer over time, to deepen the relationship, so that eventually the customer becomes an advocate for the brand.

At each stage we ask, as in the CBS, what the customer is doing, thinking and feeling, and also the contact opportunities, and how these can be developed to deepen the relationship. Because we are talking about a current customer whose profile we know throughout this process (as opposed to a consumer we are trying to acquire) there are many communications vehicles we can use to directly address them.

## What its for:

The CRS is useful in that it...

- Identifies when the role of communications is to increase loyalty and retain a customer, rather than acquire one.
- Helps fill in the Key Discoveries.
- Identifies communication vehicles which are later used in the Total Communications Plan.

## How to do it:

It can be helpful to use customer research to give insight into the nature of the relationship people have with the brand at each stage. This is best done qualitatively using short individual interviews. Don't be restricted by normal 'formal' techniques. You can often find out a considerable amount by asking people yourself, by contacting a limited number of consumers at each stage and asking basic questions. You can always quantify any insights later if this is felt necessary.

The following formats are helpful in setting down a buying system, but you can bring this to life if you use visual or audio-visual material (such as pictures and quotes from people or a camcorder video of someone going through the stages).
