Defining the best communications plan for a brand first requires a thorough analysis of the opportunities to reach a target audience at times and places when they will be most receptive to our stimulus.

The output of this section is...

- [The Communications Plan](./communications-plan.md)

There are analytical tools to help the Communications Plan development.

### [Communication Task Analysis](./communication-task-analysis.md)

Helps us to identify the intersection between our target, what we ultimately want them to do (communications objective) and where we can reach them with a stimulus.

### [Communication Roles](./communication-roles.md)

How you can tap into the consumer's relationship with the brand at different stages in the buying/ relationship process, using relevant media communication.

### [Vehicle Evaluation Grid](./vehicle-evaluation.md)

For planners who are unfamiliar with the strengths and weaknesses of the various media vehicle, this grid helps you to consider the options and relate them to the plan under formulation.

Additionally, these constructs may be helpful:

- The [Consumer Buying System](../../why-are-we-there/consumer-discoveries/consumer-buying-system/index.md) found in Why Are We There?
- The [Communications Roles and Suitablity](./communication-roles.md) found in this section.

You will find a few words of advice following this section in "Things to expect."

## Things to expect...

- Most brand teams find that writing the communications objective is the most difficult step in this process. Still, it is essential to nail this organizing statement -- as everything in your Brand Plan will be designed to support this objective.
- Getting client approval on the communications objective, communications tasks and their priority is critical.
- Initially, most Brand Plan team members feel uncertain about the relative merits of the communications tools. As a result, it is typical to find that the advertising box is checked, no matter what, or that all of the boxes are checked for every task. If this happens, review the Brand Plan grid, looking for the optimal blend of tools.
- Also, a review of the Brand Plan relative to the budget quickly reduces the number of recommended tools.
- Leave the tasks and tools intact by priority. If you do not have enough budget to accomplish everything on your Brand Plan, eliminate the lowest priority first. Continue "cuts" by elimination tasks in their entirety until the plan is affordable. Resist the temptation to "cherry pick" the tasks and tools.
