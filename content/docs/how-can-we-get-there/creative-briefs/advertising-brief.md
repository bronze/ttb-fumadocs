---
title: "Advertising Brief"
---

The most important audience for an advertising brief is the creative team. For them, a good brief is:

**Clear, coherent**...It should read like a good story

**Single-minded**... It should have real focus and ask for a single response

**Meaty**... It should contain real news, insight or facts that are helpful

**Ambitious**... It should be as ambitious as the desire for exceptional ideas

The key points to cover are:

- What is required?
- What do we expect this advertising to do?
- What helpful insight do we have about our audience?
- What single-minded response do we want from this advertising?
- What could be the best evidence to stimulate this?
- What aspects of the brand's personality are important to this advertising?
- What is the branding idea and how does it work? Are there any other branding properties that may be useful?

## Ensuring a good Creative Brief

- Encourage consumer research before the brief. This is particularly helpful in acquiring insight or arriving at the best single minded response or for honing any evidence into its most useful form.
- Use the brief as a creative stimulus not as a marketing document.
- Insist on one objective and one key response. Be realistic in setting goals.
- Be prepared to be challenged.
- Recognise the brief as the means and not the end. Allow enough time to polish the brief.
- Ensure that the brief contains some real helpful news for the creative team.

## How to make a bad Creative Brief

- Insist it reflects the marketing analysis not the creative conclusions.
- Expect the brief to be created without thorough marketing thinking.
- Put too much into it.
- Demand that the advertising should perform miracles without any evidence.
- Don't have a point of view.
- Cover your options and your backside!
