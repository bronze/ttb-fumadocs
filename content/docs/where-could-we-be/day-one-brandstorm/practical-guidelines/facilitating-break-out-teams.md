---
title: "Facilitating Break-Out Teams"
---

First of all, the composition of Break-Out teams deserves a lot of thought before the Day One session ever begins. Group dynamics need to be considered. If a strong willed senior Client will be on a particular team, a strong willed senior Agency person should be matched with them to balance them out. You also want a good mix of disciplines and seniority levels. Generally there will be four to six people in each Break-Out team depending on the size of the overall group. The senior Account and Planning people typically construct teams prior to the session.

The team should be directed to develop their Worksheet in the language of real people -our customers and users - rather than in technical terms or marketing-ese. We are also looking for descriptions of human beings, not just "consumers" of our product or service.

One critical fact that should be clear to all concerned is that any good strategic output demands sacrifice. That is, we will certainly not be able to say everything to everybody. Fairly early in the Break-Out session the team will need to decide what target, what desired behaviour, what benefit and what key support points are most important, build a strategic story around them and leave other interesting but less important issues behind.
